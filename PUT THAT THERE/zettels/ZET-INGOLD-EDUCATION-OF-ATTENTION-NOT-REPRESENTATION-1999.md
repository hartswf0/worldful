ZET-INGOLD-EDUCATION-OF-ATTENTION-NOT-REPRESENTATION-1999

TITLE:
Skill Can Be Transmitted by Educating Attention Rather Than Transmitting Complete Representations

SOURCE:
Tim Ingold - “From the Transmission of Representations to the Education of Attention” - revised 1999. ([LCHC](https://www.lchc.ucsd.edu/mca/Paper/ingold/ingold1.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
Ingold reframes cultural learning away from transmitting packaged representations and toward developing perceptual skills through guided engagement with an environment.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Prompt rituals as apprenticeship in noticing machine-relevant differences.

LOCAL MOVE:
Provides a stronger account of why prompt superstition can improve operators even when individual formulas lack stable model-side causal power.

SOURCE TERMS:
education of attention; transmission; representation; skill; environment; learning; perception.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The durable product of prompt culture may not be a library of correct phrases but an altered capacity to notice constraints, state, failure, invariants, and opportunities for repair.

QUESTION:
Does prompt expertise consist primarily in possessing better verbal templates or in perceiving interactional differences earlier?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can prompts operate as exercises that educate the human’s attention toward the behavior of probabilistic systems?

MECHANISM:
Repeated guided practice tunes perception toward features of the environment that become consequential for skilled action.

FORMAL SHIFT:
PROMPT KNOWLEDGE = REPRESENTATIONS TO MEMORIZE → PROMPT EXPERTISE = EDUCATED ATTENTION TO INTERACTION.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Ingold contrasts transmission of cultural representations with education of attention.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION - NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PROMPT_RITUAL → repeated discrimination practice → operator detects consequential differences with less explicit scaffolding.

TENSION:
The analogy could romanticize expertise unless learning effects are separated from model changes across time.

MISSING:
Longitudinal evidence that prompting practice improves transfer to unfamiliar models even when ritual phrases cease working.

BOUNDARY:
Ingold’s ecological account is not a theory of prompt engineering.

CITATION TRAIL:
Ingold → prompt superstition → operative ritual → implied/configured user.

TEST:
Train one group on fixed prompt templates and another on perturbation-and-diagnosis exercises; transfer both to a new model where the original formulas are ineffective.

PLATFORM:
Skill acquisition / human-AI interaction.
