ID:
ZET-WITTGENSTEIN-AGREEMENT-IN-JUDGMENTS-242

TITLE:
A Shared Lexicon Is Insufficient Without Enough Agreement in Judgment

SOURCE:
Ludwig Wittgenstein - Philosophical Investigations - §§240-242. ([Wiley-Blackwell](https://www.blackwellpublishing.com/content/bpl_images/content_store/sample_chapter/9780631231271/Wittgenstein.pdf))

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"'So you are saying that human agreement decides what is true and what is false?'—It is what human beings say that is true and false; and they agree in the language they use. That is not agreement in opinions, but in form of life... If language is to be a means of communication there must be agreement not only in definitions but also (queer as this may sound) in judgments." — Ludwig Wittgenstein, Philosophical Investigations, §§241–242 (1953)

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The background stability required for thin prompts to remain actionable.

LOCAL MOVE:
Qualifies the idea that prompt compression can be achieved merely by establishing vocabulary. Stable continuation also depends on recurrently compatible judgments.

SOURCE TERMS:
agreement; language; form of life; judgments; measurement; constancy.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Two agents can share the definition of “circle” yet still fail to coordinate if they repeatedly disagree over which rendered cases count as circles.

QUESTION:
What minimum judgmental agreement is required for “same,” “better,” “rounder,” or “like this” to function as compressed prompts?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is the hidden substrate of prompt fluency primarily shared vocabulary, or shared discriminatory practice?

MECHANISM:
Repeated agreement over applications stabilizes what may safely be left unstated in later utterances.

FORMAL SHIFT:
SHARED DEFINITIONS → SHARED CRITERIA OF APPLICATION.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Wittgenstein distinguishes agreement in definitions from agreement in judgments and ties measurement to constancy of results.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION - NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PROMPT_COMPRESSION requires sufficient stability of both lexical mappings and acceptance/rejection judgments.

TENSION:
Agreement in judgment enables communication but does not by itself establish external truth.

MISSING:
A measure distinguishing coordination agreement from world-grounded accuracy in human-model interaction.

BOUNDARY:
Do not turn “form of life” into a computational state variable without qualification.

CITATION TRAIL:
Philosophical Investigations §§240-242 → languaging → requisite nonalignment.

TEST:
Train two interaction histories with identical vocabulary but systematically different acceptance criteria for circle quality; test whether identical thin follow-ups diverge.

PLATFORM:
Language-game theory / evaluation.
