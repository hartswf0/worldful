TITLE:
Practices Construct the Objects Experts Learn to See

SOURCE:
Charles Goodwin - “Professional Vision” - American Anthropologist - 1994. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227980682_Professional_Vision?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
Goodwin analyzes coding schemes, highlighting, and graphic representations as socially organized practices that shape the objects of knowledge professionals perceive and contest.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Attention tax as socially trained discrimination rather than generic cognitive load.

LOCAL MOVE:
Strengthens the neo-Whorfian branch with an empirical practice account: professional languages and representations do not merely describe pre-given objects; they organize what becomes perceptually salient and professionally consequential.

SOURCE TERMS:
professional vision; coding scheme; highlighting; graphic representation; perception; object of knowledge; practice.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A distinction can become visually obvious to an expert because a professional practice has repeatedly organized attention around it.

QUESTION:
Do programming languages cultivate distinct forms of professional vision for the same artifact?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does learning SVG, Logo, GLSL, or GUI design change not only how one expresses a circle but what properties of the circle become available to perception as actionable structure?

MECHANISM:
Coding schemes partition continuous fields; highlighting makes selected differences salient; representational practices stabilize those differences as professional objects.

FORMAL SHIFT:
LANGUAGE REPRESENTS WHAT EXPERT SEES → LANGUAGE/PRACTICE PARTICIPATES IN CONSTITUTING WHAT COUNTS AS VISIBLE TO EXPERTISE.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Goodwin identifies coding schemes, highlighting, and graphic representations as practices of professional vision.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION - NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
ATTENTION_LANGUAGE_GAME(L) =
the recurrent practice through which representation L trains participants to detect some artifact differences more readily than others.

TENSION:
Professional vision is socially historical and institutionally organized; it should not be reduced to individual perceptual priming.

MISSING:
Empirical comparison of expert visual discrimination across different programming or graphics-language communities.

BOUNDARY:
Goodwin does not claim linguistic determinism.

TEST:
