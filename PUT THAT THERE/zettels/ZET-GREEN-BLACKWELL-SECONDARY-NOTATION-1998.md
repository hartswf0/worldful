ZET-GREEN-BLACKWELL-SECONDARY-NOTATION-1998

TITLE:
Operative Thickness Can Reside Outside Formal Syntax

SOURCE:
Thomas R. G. Green and Alan F. Blackwell - Cognitive Dimensions of Information Artefacts: A Tutorial - 1998. ([Cambridge Computer Lab](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~afb21/CognitiveDimensions/CDtutorial.pdf))

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"Secondary notation is information conveyed through layout, color, indentation, or spatial clustering that is not part of the formal syntax of the language, yet is vital to human comprehension. Compilers ignore secondary notation; humans rely on it." — Thomas R. G. Green and Alan F. Blackwell, Cognitive Dimensions of Information Artefacts: A Tutorial, p. 23 (1998)

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Secondary notation as a precursor to distributed prompt thickness.

LOCAL MOVE:
Provides a source-level distinction between formal syntax and information carried by presentation, directly pressuring any equation of prompt with explicit text.

SOURCE TERMS:
secondary notation; formal syntax; grouping; layout; redundant information; escape from formalism.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Information excluded from the official grammar can still be central to human parsing, checking, and successful action.

QUESTION:
When contextual information becomes directly machine-actionable, is it still “secondary notation,” or has the boundary of the language expanded?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does multimodal prompting erase the primary/secondary notation distinction by allowing gesture, layout, image, and interface state to become executable?

MECHANISM:
Additional distinctions are encoded in presentation channels not defined by the formal syntax.

FORMAL SHIFT:
PROMPT TEXT + CONTEXT → MULTICHANNEL OPERATIVE DESCRIPTION.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Secondary notation carries information outside the official syntax.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION - NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
THICKNESS = recoverable task-relevant distinctions across official syntax + secondary notation + world state + interaction history.

TENSION:
Secondary notation was developed for information artefacts and human interpretation; multimodal agents may operationalize those channels differently.

MISSING:
Empirical cases where changing only secondary notation changes an agent’s executable interpretation while semantic text remains constant.

BOUNDARY:
Do not collapse every contextual cue into “notation”; some cues belong to environment or history rather than inscription.

CITATION TRAIL:
Green and Blackwell 1998 → thick prompting → description migration.

TEST:
Keep prompt words fixed while independently perturbing layout, pointer selection, highlighted object, screen position, and code surroundings.

PLATFORM:
Notation / multimodal prompting.
