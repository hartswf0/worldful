TITLE:
The Implied Reader Is a Role the Text Requires Someone to Perform

SOURCE:
Wolfgang Iser - The Implied Reader: Patterns of Communication in Prose Fiction from Bunyan to Beckett - 1974. ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Implied_Reader.html?hl=id&id=Ix3uAAAAMAAJ&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
Iser develops literary effects through patterns of communication between text and reader; the work confronts readers with incomplete situations whose implications must be actualized in reading.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The implied user of software as an operational role rather than an imagined demographic person.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replaces the loose claim that software is “written for an imagined audience” with a stronger distinction: an artifact can structurally presuppose a role that somebody must perform for it to work.

SOURCE TERMS:
reader; role; communication; expectations; perception; interpretation; literary text.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The relevant analogue for software may not be “who the designer imagined” but “what operations the artifact requires its recipient to supply.”

QUESTION:
What user operations are structurally required for a GUI, API, prompt, or program to become executable?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can an interface possess an implied operator in the same sense that a text structures a reader role, even when no designer explicitly imagined that operator?

MECHANISM:
An artifact leaves relations incomplete and provides structures that solicit particular acts of completion from a recipient.

FORMAL SHIFT:
IMAGINED USER → STRUCTURALLY REQUIRED USER ROLE.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Iser analyzes patterns of textual communication and reader response rather than software interaction.

A software user changes machine state materially; Iser’s literary reader primarily actualizes a text in reading. The analogy is therefore not identity.
