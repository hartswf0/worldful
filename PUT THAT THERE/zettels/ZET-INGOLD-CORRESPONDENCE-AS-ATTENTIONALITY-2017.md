TITLE:
Correspondence Is Attentional Coupling, Not the Transmission of a Prior Intention

SOURCE:
Tim Ingold - “On Human Correspondence” - Journal of the Royal Anthropological Institute - 2017. ([Royal Anthropological Institute](https://rai.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/1467-9655.12541?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
Ingold defines correspondence through habit rather than volition, agencing rather than agency, and attentionality rather than intentionality; attention is described as resonant coupling of concurrent movements.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Whether prompting should be modeled as transmitting intention or as participating in unfolding correspondence.

LOCAL MOVE:
Pressures the graph’s remaining intention-first formulations. A prompt may not carry a fixed desired world forward; intention itself can transform while person, artifact, model, and environment answer one another.

SOURCE TERMS:
correspondence; habit; agencing; attentionality; intentionality; resonant coupling; answering.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A successful generative interaction may be less like executing an internal plan and more like keeping multiple trajectories sufficiently responsive to one another for a form to emerge.

QUESTION:
Can a world-to-world model support correspondence rather than merely approximate a predeclared target state?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What would an evaluation look like if the human’s desired endpoint is permitted to change legitimately during the interaction?

MECHANISM:
Participants answer to concurrent movements and environmental changes rather than simply imposing a fixed representation upon them.

FORMAL SHIFT:
INTENTION → PROGRAM → WORLD
becomes
ATTEND ↔ RESPOND ↔ CHANGING WORLD ↔ REVISED ATTENTION.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Ingold contrasts attentionality with intentionality in his theory of correspondence.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION - NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
GOAL_t is not necessarily constant;
NEXT_MOVE_t emerges from coupled trajectories of human, artifact, model, and environment.

TENSION:
Without a stable target, “success” becomes difficult to distinguish from post-hoc accommodation to whatever the system produced.

MISSING:
A criterion separating genuine emergent correspondence from preference drift induced by system output.

BOUNDARY:
Ingold writes about living beings and social life, not LLM control architectures.
