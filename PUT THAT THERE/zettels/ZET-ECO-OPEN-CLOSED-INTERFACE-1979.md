TITLE:
Interfaces Can Differ in How Narrowly They Pre-Construct Their Competent Addressee

SOURCE:
Umberto Eco - The Role of the Reader: Explorations in the Semiotics of Texts - 1979. ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Role_of_the_Reader.html?id=t55rAAAAIAAJ&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
Eco contrasts relatively open texts, which actively involve the addressee in their production, with closed texts designed to channel a more limited and predetermined response.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Open versus closed interaction grammars in software.

LOCAL MOVE:
Adds a second axis to the implied-user branch: interfaces differ not only in what competent reader they presuppose but in how tightly they constrain that reader’s possible continuations.

SOURCE TERMS:
open; closed; addressee; interpretation; Model Reader; possible world; textual cooperation.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A GUI wizard and a generative prompt box can both be easy to use while presupposing radically different degrees of interpretive cooperation.

QUESTION:
Can interface openness be separated from usability and expressive power?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does generative interaction genuinely enlarge the user’s possible language-game, or merely replace visible constraints with model-mediated invisible ones?

MECHANISM:
The artifact structures a field of possible continuations while leaving different amounts of interpretive work to the addressee.

FORMAL SHIFT:
IMPLIED USER → IMPLIED USER + WIDTH OF LICENSED CONTINUATION.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Eco’s open/closed distinction concerns texts and aesthetic interpretation.

OUR FORMALIZATION:

TENSION:
An apparently open prompt interface may be behaviorally narrow because hidden policies, model priors, or tool schemas silently constrain interpretation.

MISSING:
A metric distinguishing visible interface openness from effective operational openness.

BOUNDARY:
