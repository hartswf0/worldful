ID:
ZET-BENJAMIN-MODE-OF-INTENTION-1923

TITLE:
Translation Can Preserve an Intended Object While Changing the Mode of Intending It

SOURCE:
Walter Benjamin - “The Translator’s Task” / “The Task of the Translator.” ([Germanic Languages & Literatures](https://german.yale.edu/sites/default/files/benjamin_translators_task.pdf))

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"No poem is intended for the reader, no picture for the beholder, no symphony for the listener... The words 'Brot' and 'pain' intend the very same object, yet the modes of intending (Art des Meinens) are fundamentally different. It is in the mode of intending that the two languages diverge." — Walter Benjamin, The Task of the Translator (1923)

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Cross-language Yellow Circle implementations as transformations of operational intention rather than equivalent encodings.

LOCAL MOVE:
Refines the polyglot circle experiment: Logo, SVG, GLSL, and deixis may converge on a recognizable artifact while construing what it is to specify that artifact differently.

SOURCE TERMS:
intended object; mode of intention; kinship; translation; language.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Equivalent outputs do not imply equivalent ways of articulating or organizing the object.

QUESTION:
What survives translation across programming languages besides visible output?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a computational translation be evaluated for whether it transforms the receiving language rather than simply compiling into its pre-existing ontology?

MECHANISM:
Languages partition and articulate a shared target through non-identical systems of distinctions.

FORMAL SHIFT:
SAME MEANING IN DIFFERENT CODE → SAME TARGET, DIFFERENT MODE OF OPERATIONAL INTENTION.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Benjamin contrasts intended object with mode of intention in literary-linguistic translation.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION - NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SVG intends circle as object attributes;
Logo as movement history;
GLSL as point-membership predicate;
deixis as situated selection/action.

TENSION:
Benjamin’s theory is literary, historical, and bound to his larger philosophy of “pure language”; computational equivalence is not his subject.

MISSING:
A criterion for distinguishing a genuinely transformed receiving formalism from ordinary compilation.

BOUNDARY:
Do not treat visible artifact identity as Benjamin’s “pure language.”

CITATION TRAIL:
Benjamin → Yellow Circle polyglot corpus → description migration → worldtexting.

TEST:
Translate “put a yellow circle there” into a target language twice: first by forcing existing primitives, second by extending the target with a new deictic primitive; compare what distinctions the target subsequently makes available.

PLATFORM:
Translation theory / programming languages.
