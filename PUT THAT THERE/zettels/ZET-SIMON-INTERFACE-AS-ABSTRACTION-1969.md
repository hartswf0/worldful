TITLE:
A Simple Interface Can Hide Radical Differences in Inner Mechanism While Preserving Outer Function

SOURCE:
Herbert A. Simon - The Sciences of the Artificial - 1969/1996. ([MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262190510/the-sciences-of-the-artificial/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) ([Canonical Link](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262691918/the-sciences-of-the-artificial/))

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"An artifact can be thought of as a meeting point—an 'interface' in today's terms—between an 'inner' environment, the substance and organization of the artifact itself, and an 'outer' environment, the surroundings in which it operates... We can often predict behavior from knowledge of the system's goals and its outer environment, with only minimal assumptions about the inner environment." — Herbert A. Simon, The Sciences of the Artificial, MIT Press, pp. 6–7 (1969)

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Why radically different Yellow Circle programs can produce one stable outward artifact.

LOCAL MOVE:
Supplies a design-theoretic alternative to claiming that all Yellow Circle languages represent one underlying ontology.

SOURCE TERMS:
inner environment; outer environment; interface; functional description; abstraction; generality; adaptation.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Similarity at the interface can coexist with essential internal difference; the shared circle need not reveal or privilege the machinery that produced it.

QUESTION:
Is the Yellow Circle experiment fundamentally an interface-equivalence experiment rather than an ontology-equivalence experiment?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How much internal representational heterogeneity can be hidden behind one stable world-facing invariant?

MECHANISM:
Different internal organizations can adapt to the same outer task constraints and expose equivalent interface behavior.

FORMAL SHIFT:
SAME OUTPUT IMPLIES SHARED REPRESENTATION → SAME OUTER FUNCTION CAN MASK NON-ISOMORPHIC INNER MECHANISMS.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Simon factors artificial systems into goals, inner environment, outer environment, and their interface.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION - NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SVG, Logo, GLSL, Paint, and Astra are equivalent only relative to a chosen acceptance interface:
ACCEPT(rendered_state) = YELLOW_CIRCLE.

TENSION:
A coarse acceptance interface can hide differences that become consequential under later transformations.

MISSING:
A hierarchy of acceptance interfaces for identity, editability, provenance, and continuation.

BOUNDARY:
Functional equivalence relative to one task does not establish semantic or representational equivalence.

CITATION TRAIL:
Simon 1969 → Yellow Circle invariance → Brooks conceptual integrity → boundary objects.

TEST:
First compare languages only on rendered output; then demand editability, scaling, recoloring, and referential persistence to reveal previously hidden inner differences.

PLATFORM:
Design theory / programming languages.
