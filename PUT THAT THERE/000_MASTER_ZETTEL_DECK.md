**Title:** The Builder’s Game Already Contains THERE, THIS, Pointing, and Colour  
**Source:** Ludwig Wittgenstein - Philosophical Investigations - §8. ([Wiley-Blackwell](https://www.blackwellpublishing.com/content/bpl_images/content_store/sample_chapter/9780631231271/Wittgenstein.pdf))  
**Platform:** `Philosophy of language / HCI genealogy.`  

**Primary Passage (Verbatim Citation):**
> [QUOTE]
> "Let us now consider an expansion of language (2). Besides the four words 'block', 'pillar', etc., let it contain a series of words used as the shopkeeper used the numerals (it may be the series of letters of the alphabet); further, two words, which may be 'there' and 'this' (because this roughly indicates their purpose), and which are used in connection with a pointing gesture; and finally a number of colour samples. A gives an order like: 'd—slab—there'. At the same time he shows the assistant a colour sample, and when he says 'there' he points to a place on the building site. **Research Object:** The formal resemblance between Wittgenstein’s expanded builder language and Put-That-There.  

**Mechanism:** Speech leaves location and object selection incomplete; gesture supplies those distinctions inside a trained practice.
