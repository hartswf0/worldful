VERSE 3 • THE STICKY FINGERS OF MEMORY
Years teach objects second meanings. Trampoline turns monument. Flower turns archive. Cup turns relic. Road turns exile. Memory got sticky fingers. Comes back grown, moves everything. Calls rearrangement “remembering.” I say “before.” She says “before.” Same word, different floor. Her before got parents. Mine got towers. Both got doors we can’t walk through anymore. That’s time travel: not going back. Going back with what you know now. Future in your pocket like contraband. Past at customs: Anything to declare? Yeah. Everything. Nothing was this heavy there.
BRIDGE & FINAL VERSE • CONTACT & GRAMMAR
A date is a room after people leave. A word is a bridge missing one plank. A calendar is a map arguing with the ground. A child is a witness without the language to testify. So when she says መልካም አዲስ ዓመት, I hear more than “happy new year.” I hear: you survived to rename the morning. I hear: your dead do not own every flower. I hear: my history was never the whole date. Same sun. Different sentence. Same earth. Different emphasis. And somehow two kids with no word for each other grew into people trying to translate air. That’s the miracle. Not accuracy. Contact. Not one truth. Overlap. Not perfect speech. A word crosses. Comes back changed. Still lands.

II. The "Put That There" Archive & Methodological Hinge

Richard Bolt (1980) + Ludwig Wittgenstein (1953 §8). Why language force does not live inside the sentence.

ROOT REPOSITORY: /PUT THAT THERE/
HISTORICAL MILESTONE • 1980 MIT ARCHITECTURE MACHINE GROUP
RICHARD BOLT: "PUT THAT THERE"

In 1980, Richard Bolt demonstrated voice and gesture at the graphics interface. A user wearing a Polhemus 6-DOF magnetic wrist tracker points at a cathode-ray projection screen and speaks: “Create a blue square there... Put that there.”

The Discovery: The sentence “Put that there” contains almost zero descriptive information. There are no spatial coordinates, no RGB codes, no object IDs. It works because the operative distinctions are carried by the physical co-presence of hand, voice, screen, timing, and an established response practice.

PHILOSOPHICAL INVARIANT • 1953 PHILOSOPHICAL INVESTIGATIONS §8
WITTGENSTEIN: "d—slab—there"

In §8 of the Philosophical Investigations, Ludwig Wittgenstein expands his famous builder's game: Builder A calls out an order: “d—slab—there.” He shows a colour swatch and points to a place on the building site.

The Structural Collision: Resemblance is not influence: this is an epistemological collision. Both 1953 and 1980 prove that linguistic force does not reside in the text string. Pointing collapses O(N) taxonomic coordinate description into O(1) situated coupling.

OPERATIONAL ONTOLOGY

The Deictic Aperture Principle

The prevailing modern paradigm of “prompt engineering” treats language as an autonomous command string dumped into a disembodied box. When natural language computing collapsed dozens of communicative forms into an undifferentiated input rectangle, it severed language from its spatial and indexical grounding.

“A prompt is merely an under-specified move inside an unacknowledged game. Prompt thickness is not word count; it is how much of what is necessary for action is recoverable from the coupled situation.”
